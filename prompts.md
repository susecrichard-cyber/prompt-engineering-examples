# Prompt patterns

Replace the bracketed parts with your own topic or text.

## 1. Ask

> Answer this question directly, in plain language: [question]
> If you are unsure, say so and explain what would settle it.

Why it works: it asks for a direct answer and for uncertainty to be stated, not guessed.

## 2. Explain

> Explain [topic] to someone who has never seen it before.
> Start with the core idea in plain language, define any technical term as it appears, and use one concrete example.

Why it works: it sets the audience, the order, and the example in advance.

## 3. Brainstorm

> Give me 10 distinct, concrete ideas for [goal].
> Group them under short headings, do not repeat the same idea in different words, and close with which 2 look strongest and why.

Why it works: it asks for variety, grouping, and a short ranking at the end.

## 4. Draft

> Draft a [type of text, e.g. email, summary, message] about [subject].
> Keep it polite and concrete, under [number] words, and in the tone of [tone].

Why it works: format, subject, length, and tone are all given up front.

## 5. Plan

> Make a plan to [goal]. Break it into ordered, concrete steps, say what each step depends on, and flag any decision I still need to make.

Why it works: it asks for order, dependencies, and open decisions.

## 6. Summarize

> Summarize the text below. Lead with the bottom line, then the supporting points, then anything unresolved. Do not add facts that are not in the text.
>
> [paste the text here]

Why it works: it sets the structure and forbids adding facts.
